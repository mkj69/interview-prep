研究讨论轮：像研究者一样说话

Coding 轮考你能不能写，知识轮考你懂不懂，研究讨论轮（research discussion / research deep-dive）考的是：你是不是一个面试官愿意每周一起开组会的人。这一章把这类面试拆成可以练习的动作：讲论文、讲自己的研究、批判论文、现场提 idea、聊研究品味，并配一份核实过链接的必读论文清单和三段完整的英文模拟对话。

这一章的目标。学完以后你应该能：(1) 用 5 分钟、固定结构讲清楚任何一篇论文（问题 → 方法 → 证据 → 局限 → 你的看法）；(2) 用一个通用模板准备 20 分钟和 45 分钟的研究 talk，并提前写好最难的 10 个追问的答案；(3) 用一张检查表在 10 分钟内找出一篇论文最重要的 3 个问题，并且说得礼貌、具体、可检验；(4) 在被问 "what would you do in a 12-week internship?" 时，按 "瓶颈 → 假设 → 最小实验 → 指标 → 风险 → 如何放大" 现场搭出一个可信的项目；(5) 熟悉 60 篇常被引用的论文的一句话贡献和常见追问。

建议顺序：§1（知道在考什么）→ §2（讲论文，最常见）→ §5（提 idea，最能拉开差距）→ §4（批判）→ §3（准备自己的 talk）→ §6 / §8（品味与开放问题）→ §9（大声读模拟对话，最好录音）。§7 的论文清单当作词典，按需查。

和其他模块的关系：这里不重复技术细节——DPO / GRPO 的推导在 06，test-time compute 在 07，agents 在 10，可解释性在 11，评估与实验设计在 13。本章教你如何在讨论中使用这些知识；英文表达的通用短语库在 18。

§1 · WHAT IS BEING TESTED研究轮在考什么

先弄清楚"考卷"长什么样。不同公司、不同组的叫法和形式差别很大，下面只总结常见的模式；具体到某个面试，最可靠的信息来源永远是 recruiter——直接问"这一轮的形式是什么、多长、要不要准备 slides"完全正常。

★

研究轮的五种常见形式

一句话

研究轮大致有五种：讲自己的研究（talk）、深挖讨论（deep-dive）、读/批判一篇论文（critique）、现场提项目（propose）、研究品味问答（taste）；实际面试常常是几种混合。

Research rounds usually mix five formats: your talk, a deep-dive, a paper critique, a live project proposal, and research-taste questions.

形式典型样子面试官真正想看到的准备方式
Job talk / research talk20–45 分钟讲你的研究，常有 slides，后面 Q&A；有时面向整个组能不能把问题讲得"值得做"；能不能分清你的贡献和别人的；结果是否可信§3 模板；提前排练 3 遍以上，计时
Deep-dive没有 slides，面试官挑你简历上一个项目一路往下追问："为什么这样设计？""如果去掉 X 会怎样？"你是否真正理解自己做的东西（而不是跟着导师/代码跑）；对失败和局限的诚实度对每个项目准备"设计决策表"和"最难 10 问"（§3）
Paper discussion / critique提前给一篇论文，或现场请你讲一篇你喜欢的论文，然后讨论优缺点读论文的速度和深度；能否区分"论文声称的"和"证据支持的"；批评是否具体、建设性§2 讲论文模板 + §4 批判检查表
Project proposal"如果你来我们组 12 周，你会做什么？""怎么提升 X？"问题拆解能力、对可行性的判断、是否会设计能出结论的最小实验§5 框架 + 6 个范例
Research taste"最近最被高估的方向是什么？""你认为 scaling 还能走多远？"是否有自己的、有依据的观点；能否同时看到反方论据；会不会人云亦云§6 框架 + §8 开放问题
直觉把研究轮想象成"试开一次组会"。面试官在模拟：如果这个人下周出现在我们的 meeting 上，讲他的进展、评论别人的结果、提出下一步，我会不会觉得有收获？所有技巧都服务于这个画面。

实习（intern）面试和全职面试相比，通常更看重潜力和合作感：面试官知道一年级博士生的成果有限，更关心你学得快不快、想得清不清楚、能不能在 12 周内交付一个有结论的东西。所以"成果不多"不是致命问题，"说不清楚"才是。

常见误区（1）只准备 talk，忽视 deep-dive——很多面试根本不让你放 slides。（2）把研究轮当成知识考试，背了很多事实但没有观点。（3）以为必须讲自己最"厉害"的项目；其实应该讲你理解最深、能经得起追问的项目。
Say it in English

Before the interview, it's completely fine to ask the recruiter: "Could you tell me a bit more about the format of the research round? Should I prepare slides, and roughly how long is the presentation part versus the discussion?" In my experience these rounds are basically a trial lab meeting — the interviewer wants to see how I explain a problem, how I defend design decisions, and how I react to new ideas.

追问链
  1. Which of your projects would you like to talk about, and why that one?
    思路
    选标准：你主导了关键决策、能讲出"为什么不用别的方法"、有至少一个负面结果或意外发现。回答结构："I'd like to talk about X, because it's the project where I made most of the key decisions, and it has a clear story: [problem] → [what we tried] → [what surprised us]."
  2. How much of this was your contribution versus your collaborators'?
    思路
    提前想好，诚实而具体："I designed the training setup and ran all the ablations; my collaborator built the evaluation harness; the original idea came out of a discussion with my advisor." 说"we"可以，但被问到时必须能拆开。含糊其辞是大忌。
★

面试官在找的信号（和反信号）

一句话

核心信号是：清晰（能讲明白）、深度（知道为什么）、诚实（分清证据与猜测）、品味（知道什么值得做）、可合作（被挑战时开放而不防御）。

They look for clarity, depth, intellectual honesty, taste, and how you respond to pushback.

下面是一份通用的信号清单（总结自常见的研究面试评价维度，并非任何一家公司的官方 rubric）：

维度正信号反信号
清晰度先给结论再给细节；会用一个具体例子说明抽象问题；会画图从实现细节开始讲；满屏术语；10 分钟还没说清楚问题是什么
深度 / first principles能解释方法为什么有效，能预测改动某个因素会发生什么只会复述论文里的步骤；"因为 paper 是这么做的"
实验素养主动提 baseline、ablation、seed 方差、算力匹配（compute-matched）比较、污染检查只看一个数字；不知道结果的误差范围
诚实"I don't know, but here's how I'd find out"；主动说出局限编造；夸大贡献；被指出问题时硬撑
品味能说明为什么这个问题重要、与领域大趋势的关系；知道什么时候该放弃一个方向做的东西只是"把 A 和 B 拼起来"，说不出意义
合作感把面试官的质疑当成共同解决问题；"That's a good point — one way to test that would be…"防御、打断、把讨论变成辩论比赛
执行力能估算一个实验需要多少 GPU 小时、多少天；知道先做什么最小实验想法很大但无法落地；不知道第一周做什么
直觉面试官在 45 分钟里无法验证你的研究是否正确，但能很好地观察你如何思考。所以"思考过程可见"比"答案完美"重要——边想边说（think out loud）、把假设说出来、说出你打算怎么检验。
常见误区把"被挑战"理解为"我答错了"。研究面试官经常故意反驳，看你是会坚持有证据的观点，还是一推就倒，还是硬撑。最好的反应是：先承认对方观点中合理的部分，再说明你的证据，最后提出一个能分辨两种观点的实验。
Say it in English

That's a fair challenge. I think you're right that our gains could partly come from the longer training schedule rather than the method itself. We did run a compute-matched baseline, and the gap shrank from about five points to three, so I believe some of the effect is real — but if I were to redo it, I'd also add a run where the baseline gets the same number of gradient steps on the same data.

追问链
  1. What would convince you that your approach is wrong?
    思路
    这是测试"可证伪性"思维。给出一个具体的、可执行的失败条件："If the improvement disappears under a compute-matched baseline, or doesn't transfer to a second model family, I'd conclude it's an artifact of our setup." 能说出这句话本身就是强信号。

两周准备计划（研究轮专用）

一句话

研究轮靠"说出来"的练习，不靠读：每天至少 30 分钟用英文出声讲论文或讲 idea，并录音回听。

Practice out loud in English every day and record yourself — reading alone doesn't transfer to speaking.

天任务产出

Say it in English

Before I interview with a team, I usually read their last two or three papers and try to come up with one concrete question and one possible follow-up for each. It makes the conversation much more specific, and it also helps me figure out whether my interests actually overlap with what they're working on.

§2 · PRESENTING A PAPER讲论文：5 分钟模板

"Tell me about a paper you read recently that you liked" 几乎是必考题。好答案的结构是固定的：先让听者知道为什么要关心，再讲核心想法（一个直觉 + 一个公式），再讲证据，最后给出你自己的看法——最后这一步是区分"读过"和"想过"的关键。

★

5 分钟讲论文模板（PROBLEM–IDEA–EVIDENCE–LIMITS–TAKE）

一句话

按 问题（30s）→ 之前的做法为什么不够（30s）→ 核心想法（90s）→ 关键证据（60s）→ 局限（45s）→ 我的看法与后续（45s）讲，先给一句话总结。

Headline first, then problem, gap, key idea, evidence, limitations, and your own take.

段落时间要回答的问题英文开头句
0. Headline15s一句话：这篇论文做了什么、为什么重要"The one-sentence version is: …"
1. Problem30s要解决什么问题？为什么重要？"The problem they tackle is … This matters because …"
2. Gap30s现有方法哪里不够（贵、不稳定、不可扩展、有偏差）？"The standard approach is …, but it has two issues: …"
3. Key idea90s核心洞察是什么？用一个直觉 + 最多一个公式"Their key insight is that … Concretely, …"
4. Evidence60s最有说服力的 1–2 个实验；和什么比；提升多少"The most convincing result, in my view, is …"
5. Limits45s证据没覆盖什么？哪些假设可能不成立？"What I'm less sure about is …"
6. My take45s你怎么看？它改变了什么？你会做什么后续实验？"If I were to follow up, I'd …"

选论文的标准：(1) 你能在不看论文的情况下讲出核心公式；(2) 有一个清晰的"aha"洞察；(3) 和你的研究或目标组相关；(4) 你对它有一个具体的、非显然的批评或后续想法。经典论文（DPO、Chinchilla、CoT）安全但难以出彩；最近 6 个月的论文能体现你跟进前沿，但要确保你真的读透了——面试官可能比你更熟。

直觉听众的注意力在前 30 秒最高。如果你一开始就讲实现细节，面试官会在脑中挣扎"这到底要解决什么？"。Headline 先行等于给听众一张地图。
常见误区（1）按论文章节顺序复述（Abstract → Related work → …），没有重点；（2）讲了 5 分钟方法，没时间讲你的看法；（3）只说优点，被问"有什么缺点"时卡住；（4）数字记错——不确定就说"roughly"或"a few points"，不要编。
Say it in English

Sure — let me start with the one-sentence version, then go into the details. The paper shows that you can do preference fine-tuning without training a separate reward model or running RL, by turning the RLHF objective into a simple classification loss on preference pairs. I'll explain why that works, what evidence they give, and then where I think it breaks down.

追问链
  1. Why did you pick this paper?
    思路
    给出两个理由：内在理由（洞察优雅、改变了领域做法）+ 个人理由（和你研究的联系）。"Partly because the core trick is really elegant, and partly because it changed how I think about [your area]."
  2. What's the one experiment you wish they had run?
    思路
    这是最常见的追问，一定要提前准备。好答案是针对论文最关键假设的实验，而不是"更多数据集"。例如对 DPO："a compute- and data-matched comparison against a well-tuned PPO baseline at larger scale, with on-policy data."

英文短语库：讲论文与讨论

一句话

准备好一组"路标短语"（signposting）和"不确定性短语"（hedging），让你在思考时也能保持流畅。

Signposting and calibrated hedging phrases keep you fluent while you think.

场景短语
开场 / 路标"Let me give you the big picture first, then zoom in." · "There are three parts to this." · "The key idea is…" · "To make this concrete, …" · "Going back to your earlier question, …"
解释直觉"The intuition is…" · "One way to think about it is…" · "If you squint, this is basically…" · "A useful analogy is…"
描述证据"They compare against … under a matched compute budget." · "The gain is consistent across …" · "The effect size is modest — roughly … — but it holds across seeds."
校准的不确定性"I'm fairly confident that…" · "My understanding is…, but I might be misremembering the exact number." · "I'd guess…, though I haven't verified it."
礼貌地批评"One thing I wasn't fully convinced by is…" · "I'd be curious whether this still holds when…" · "An alternative explanation could be…" · "This is a minor point, but…"
回应质疑"That's a fair point." · "I agree that's a weakness; the way I'd address it is…" · "I see it slightly differently, because…" · "I think we're actually saying the same thing from different angles."
争取时间"Let me think about that for a second." · "That's a great question — let me make sure I understand it: are you asking whether…?" · "Can I sketch this on the whiteboard?"
收尾"So, to summarize: …" · "Does that answer your question, or should I go deeper on…?"

发音和节奏建议：说慢一点（比你觉得的慢 20%），每段结束停顿；术语读准（Bradley–Terry, KL divergence, ablation, heuristic）；用"so"、"basically"适量连接，但不要每句都用。

Say it in English

One thing I wasn't fully convinced by is the evaluation. They use GPT-4 as the judge, which is reasonable, but I'd be curious whether the ranking still holds with human raters, especially for longer responses, since model judges are known to prefer longer answers.

处理 "I don't know"

一句话

不知道时：承认 → 说出你知道的相邻事实 → 给出有理有据的猜测并标注置信度 → 说你会如何验证。绝不编造。

Admit it, anchor on what you do know, reason to a calibrated guess, and say how you'd check.

面试官经常故意把问题推到你知识的边界之外——他们想看的是你在边界处的行为。四步法：

  1. 承认（Acknowledge）："I haven't read that paper in detail." / "I don't remember the exact number."
  2. 锚定（Anchor）："What I do know is that…"——把问题连到你熟悉的东西。
  3. 推理（Reason）："If I had to guess, I'd expect…, because…"——显示推理过程，并说明置信度。
  4. 验证（Verify）："The way I'd check is…"——一个具体的实验或查找方法。

例子：被问"Do you know how much compute DeepSeek-R1's RL stage used?"——"I don't think the exact number for the RL stage is something I can recall reliably. What I know is that the base model, DeepSeek-V3, is a mixture-of-experts model with around 37B active parameters, and RL with long rollouts is dominated by generation cost. So I'd reason about it as (number of RL steps) × (rollouts per step) × (average response length) × (cost per generated token), and I'd check their report for those quantities rather than guess a total."

直觉研究工作的大部分时间都在"不知道"的状态里。面试官要的不是百科全书，而是一个在不确定中仍然能有条理地前进、并且不会自欺欺人的合作者。编造一个数字被戳穿，比说"不知道"严重一百倍。
常见误区（1）沉默太久——超过 10 秒就要开口，说出你在想什么；（2）连续说三次"I don't know"不给任何推理；（3）过度道歉。简短承认后立刻进入推理。
Say it in English

I'm honestly not sure — I haven't worked with that method directly. But my guess is that it would struggle in the same place most off-policy methods do: once the policy drifts away from the data distribution, the preference signal becomes less informative. If I wanted to check, I'd track how the likelihood of the chosen responses evolves during training; if it drops a lot, that would support this explanation.

完整范例：用英文 5 分钟讲 DPO

一句话

DPO 把"KL 约束下最大化奖励"的最优策略写成闭式解，反解出 reward = β·log(π/π_ref)+常数，代入 Bradley–Terry 偏好模型后常数抵消，得到一个只需要偏好对的分类损失。

DPO reparameterizes the reward through the optimal KL-regularized policy, so the partition function cancels in the Bradley–Terry likelihood and RLHF becomes a classification loss.

论文：Rafailov et al., Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model (2023)。下面先给出你必须能在白板上写出来的推导（中文注释），再给完整的英文讲稿。推导的数值验证（闭式最优解、损失与梯度）已在 numpy 中检查。

白板推导（必须能默写）

Step 1：RLHF 目标。给定 prompt \(x\)，奖励 \(r(x,y)\)，参考策略 \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\)：

$$\max_{\pi}\; \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi(\cdot|x)}\big[r(x,y)\big] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot|x)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\big)$$

Step 2：闭式最优解。把目标写成 \(-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi\,\|\,\tfrac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\text{ref}}e^{r/\beta}\big)+\beta\log Z(x)\)，KL 在两个分布相等时取最小值 0，所以

$$\pi^*(y|x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{r(x,y)}{\beta}\Big),\qquad Z(x)=\sum_y \pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{r(x,y)}{\beta}\Big).$$

Step 3：反解 reward。两边取对数：

$$r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}+\beta\log Z(x).$$

\(Z(x)\) 要对所有可能的回复求和，无法计算——但它只依赖 \(x\)。

Step 4：代入 Bradley–Terry。偏好模型 \(p(y_w\succ y_l|x)=\sigma\big(r(x,y_w)-r(x,y_l)\big)\)。两个回复共享同一个 \(x\)，\(\beta\log Z(x)\) 相减抵消：

$$p(y_w\succ y_l|x)=\sigma\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)}\Big).$$

Step 5：把 \(\pi^*\) 换成可训练的 \(\pi_\theta\)，做最大似然：

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\theta)=-\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\Big[\log\sigma\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)}\Big)\Big].$$

Step 6：梯度（解释"它在做什么"）。记隐式奖励 \(\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}\)，利用 \(\frac{d}{dz}\log\sigma(z)=\sigma(-z)\)：

$$\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}=-\beta\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\sigma\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\big)\big(\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_w|x)-\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_l|x)\big)\Big].$$

含义：提高 \(y_w\) 的概率、降低 \(y_l\) 的概率；权重 \(\sigma(\hat r_l-\hat r_w)\) 在模型"排错序"时大、排对时小——这是自带的 hard-example weighting。初始化时 \(\pi_\theta=\pi_{\text{ref}}\)，损失恰好是 \(\log 2\)。

注意：梯度只约束 差值，所以训练中 \(\log\pi_\theta(y_w)\) 和 \(\log\pi_\theta(y_l)\) 可能同时下降，只要 \(y_l\) 降得更快（这就是后续工作讨论的 "chosen likelihood decreasing" 问题）。

# numpy check of the DPO loss / gradient (run in scratchpad/m16/checks.py)
import numpy as np
def logsig(x): return -np.logaddexp(0, -x)
def dpo_loss(pw, pl, rw, rl, beta=0.1):          # summed log-probs per sequence
    z = beta * ((pw - rw) - (pl - rl))
    return -logsig(z).mean()
# at init (policy == reference) the loss is exactly log 2
rw, rl = np.array([-10.5, -12.]), np.array([-10.5, -11.])
assert abs(dpo_loss(rw, rl, rw, rl) - np.log(2)) < 1e-12

Model answer · DPO

[Headline] The paper I'd like to talk about is DPO, Direct Preference Optimization, from Stanford in 2023. The one-sentence version: you can do RLHF-style preference tuning without training a separate reward model and without running RL, by turning the problem into a simple classification loss on pairs of responses.

[Problem & gap] The standard RLHF pipeline, as in InstructGPT, has three stages: supervised fine-tuning, fitting a reward model on human preference pairs, and then optimizing the policy against that reward with PPO while keeping a KL penalty to the reference model. The last stage is the painful one — you need to sample from the policy during training, keep a value network around, and PPO is notoriously sensitive to hyperparameters.

[Key idea] The key insight is that the KL-regularized objective has a closed-form optimal policy: the reference policy reweighted by the exponentiated reward, divided by a partition function. If you invert that, the reward can be written as beta times the log-ratio between the optimal policy and the reference, plus a term that only depends on the prompt. Now, the Bradley–Terry preference model only cares about the difference in rewards between two responses to the same prompt, so that prompt-dependent term — the intractable partition function — cancels out. What you're left with is a logistic loss on the difference of log-ratios for the chosen and rejected responses. In other words, the language model itself is implicitly the reward model, which is where the title comes from.

[Evidence] They evaluate on controlled sentiment generation, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. The result I find most convincing is the sentiment task, because there they have a ground-truth reward and can plot reward against KL to the reference: DPO gets a better trade-off frontier than PPO at the same KL. On summarization and dialogue they use GPT-4 as a judge and report win rates that are competitive with or better than their PPO baseline.

[Limits] What I'm less sure about is how general the comparison is. First, DPO is trained on a fixed, offline preference dataset, whereas PPO samples on-policy; later work, for example a 2024 ICML study by Xu and colleagues, argues that a carefully tuned PPO can outperform DPO, especially when the preference data is out of distribution for the policy. Second, because the loss only depends on the margin, the likelihood of the chosen response can actually go down during training, which several follow-ups observed. And third, the evaluation in the original paper relies heavily on model-based judges at relatively small scale.

[My take] I still think it's a really important paper: it made preference optimization accessible to anyone who can do supervised fine-tuning, and it reframed the reward model as something implicit in the policy. If I were to follow up, I'd want a clean, compute-matched comparison between offline DPO, iterative or online DPO where you regenerate pairs from the current policy, and PPO — and I'd track not just win rate but how far the policy drifts in KL, to separate "better optimization" from "just moving further from the reference."

常见误区（1）说"DPO 不需要 reward"——准确说法是不需要显式训练的 reward model，reward 被隐式参数化在策略里；（2）忘记 \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) 在训练中是冻结的；（3）说 DPO 一定比 PPO 好——证据是混合的，说"depends on data and setup"并给出理由才是成熟答案。
追问链
  1. What does β control, and what happens if it's too small?
    思路
    \(\beta\) 是 KL 约束强度：\(\beta\) 大 → 策略贴近参考模型；\(\beta\) 小 → 允许偏离更多、更"相信"偏好数据，容易过拟合偏好数据、出现退化（例如回复变长、重复）。在损失里 \(\beta\) 也缩放 logit，影响 sigmoid 的饱和速度。
  2. Why can the log-probability of the chosen response decrease?
    思路
    损失只依赖 \(\hat r_w-\hat r_l\)。当 \(y_w\) 和 \(y_l\) 很相似（共享很多 token）时，降低共享 token 的概率会同时降低两者；只要 \(y_l\) 降得更多，损失仍然下降。Smaug（Pal et al. 2024, arXiv 2402.13228）从理论和实验上分析了这一点，并提出 DPO-Positive 加一项惩罚。
  3. Is DPO on-policy or off-policy? Why does that matter?
    思路
    标准 DPO 在固定数据集上训练，是 offline / off-policy 的。问题：数据中的回复可能来自别的模型，策略在自己会生成的回复上得不到信号；随训练推进分布偏移越来越大。缓解：iterative/online DPO（周期性用当前策略生成新对并标注）。
  4. Does DPO avoid reward over-optimization?
    思路
    不能完全避免。Rafailov et al. 2024（Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization in Direct Alignment Algorithms）报告：direct alignment 方法同样出现过度优化，在 KL 预算较大时尤其明显，而且常在训练不到一个 epoch 时就开始退化。

第二个范例：90 秒讲 Chinchilla（短版也要会）

一句话

Chinchilla 发现固定算力下，参数量和训练 token 应该大致等比例增长（约 20 token/参数），因此当时的大模型普遍"训练不足"；70B 的 Chinchilla 用和 280B Gopher 相同的算力、4 倍数据，表现更好。

For a fixed compute budget, parameters and tokens should scale roughly equally — about 20 tokens per parameter — so many large models were undertrained.

论文：Hoffmann et al., Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (2022)。要点：

  • 训练了 400 多个模型（70M 到 16B+ 参数，5B 到 500B token），用三种方法估计 compute-optimal 的 \(N_{\text{opt}}(C)\) 和 \(D_{\text{opt}}(C)\)：(1) 固定模型大小、变化 token 数，取每个算力下损失包络线；(2) IsoFLOP 曲线；(3) 拟合参数化损失 \(L(N,D)=E+\frac{A}{N^\alpha}+\frac{B}{D^\beta}\)。
  • 结论：\(N_{\text{opt}}\propto C^{a}\)，\(D_{\text{opt}}\propto C^{b}\)，\(a\approx b\approx 0.5\)。对比 Kaplan et al. 2020 给出的 \(N_{\text{opt}}\propto C^{0.73}\)（偏向"把算力花在更大的模型上"）。
  • 估算：用 \(C\approx 6ND\) 和 \(D\approx 20N\)，得到 \(N\approx\sqrt{C/120}\)。代入 Chinchilla 的算力（与 Gopher 相同，约 \(6\times 280\text{B}\times 300\text{B}\approx 5\times10^{23}\) FLOPs）得 \(N\approx 65\text{–}70\)B、\(D\approx 1.3\text{–}1.4\)T，和实际的 70B / 1.4T 吻合（numpy 已验证）。

后续与批评（讨论时加分）：

  • Besiroglu et al. 2024，Chinchilla Scaling: A replication attempt：从论文图中重建数据重新拟合方法 3，发现原文报告的参数拟合与数据不符、置信区间过窄；他们重新拟合后的结果与方法 1、2 一致。所以"20 token/参数"这个主结论仍然站得住，但方法 3 的具体系数不可靠。
  • Porian et al. 2024，Resolving Discrepancies in Compute-Optimal Scaling of Language Models：把 Kaplan 与 Chinchilla 的差异归因于最后一层计算量的计入、warmup 长度、以及随模型规模调整优化器超参等因素。
  • Sardana et al. 2023，Beyond Chinchilla-Optimal：如果考虑推理成本，应训练更小的模型、用远多于 20 token/参数的数据——这解释了为什么现在的开源模型普遍"过度训练"。
Say it in English

Chinchilla asked a simple question: if you have a fixed compute budget, how should you split it between model size and training tokens? They trained over four hundred models and found that parameters and tokens should grow roughly in proportion — about twenty tokens per parameter. That implied models like Gopher were heavily undertrained, and they showed it by training a 70B model on four times more data with the same compute, which beat the 280B Gopher. The caveat I'd add is that "compute-optimal" only counts training compute; if you care about serving cost, you want to train smaller models for much longer, which is exactly what most open models do today.

追问链
  1. Why did Kaplan et al. get a different exponent?
    思路
    常见解释：Kaplan 的小模型实验中学习率 schedule 没有随训练长度调整（固定 cosine 长度），导致短训练的模型被低估；以及参数计数（是否包含 embedding）、最后一层计算等口径问题。Porian et al. 2024 系统复现并定位了几个因素。回答时说"several methodological differences, notably…"即可，不要说成单一原因。
  2. Does the law still apply when data is limited?
    思路
    不完全。Muennighoff et al. 2023（Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models）研究了重复数据：重复几个 epoch 的收益接近新数据，之后收益迅速递减。数据受限时最优分配会向更多参数/更多 epoch 偏移。

1. 先公平地复述（steelman）

  • 主张：RLVR（用可验证奖励做 RL）训练出的模型，能解决的问题集合基本是 base model 在足够多次采样下就能解决的子集；RL 提高的是 pass@1（采样效率），不是能力边界。
  • 证据：在数学（GSM8K / MATH500 / AIME24）、代码（LiveCodeBench、HumanEval+ 等）和视觉推理任务上，跨多个模型家族，RL 模型在 k 小时 pass@k 更高，但随着 k 增大到几十、几百，base model 追平并反超。作者还指出蒸馏（从更强模型学习）能引入 base model 没有的推理模式。
  • 已经处理的质疑：AIME 答案是小整数，大 k 时可能"蒙对"——作者用代码任务（单元测试很难蒙过）显示同样趋势，并人工检查了 base model 的 CoT，报告通常能找到至少一条有效推理路径。

2. 优点

  • 问题重要且表述清晰：把"RL 是否教会新东西"变成一个可以测量的问题。
  • 跨任务、跨模型家族、跨 RL 算法的系统比较，而不是单个 cherry-picked 设置。
  • 主动处理了最明显的反驳（猜答案）。

3. 主要疑问（按重要性排序）

  1. pass@k（大 k）是否等于"能力"？在 k = 256 时 base model 能解出一道题，意味着正确答案在它的分布里有非零概率——但在实际使用中，没有 verifier 就无法从 256 个样本中挑出正确的那个。所以"能力边界"的定义本身是有争议的：一个更实用的定义可能是"在可承受的采样预算内、无需 oracle 就能可靠解决"。这个疑问不推翻论文的观察，但限制了它的解读。
  2. RL 训练的长度和规模。如果 RL 只训练了相对较少的步数、数据多样性有限，那么"边界不扩展"可能只是"还没训练够"。后续工作 ProRL（Liu et al. 2025, Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models）报告：长时间、多样化任务的 RL 训练能让模型找到 base model 大量采样也找不到的策略。两篇论文的结论并不一定矛盾——它们可能在描述不同训练机制下的行为。
  3. 对 base model 的依赖。部分 RLVR 现象对模型家族高度敏感。例如 Shao et al. 2025（Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR）发现：在 Qwen2.5-Math 上，即使使用随机或弱相关的奖励，GRPO 也能提升数学分数，但这些收益没有迁移到 Llama3 或 OLMo2。这提示：在某些 base model 上，RL 主要在"激发"预训练已有的行为，这与 Yue et al. 的论点一致，但也说明结论可能依赖于预训练分布，不能直接推广到所有模型。
  4. 采样温度等评估细节。pass@k 对温度、top-p 很敏感：RL 后的模型熵通常更低，在同一温度下多样性更差。比较两个模型的大 k 表现时，理想做法是各自在最优采样设置下比较。（这是一个应当检查的问题，不是对论文的断言——提问时说 "I'd want to check whether…"。）

4. 我会提出的实验

  • 按 RL 训练步数画"覆盖率曲线"：base 能解但 RL 模型不能解的题、RL 模型能解但 base 不能解的题，各有多少，随训练如何变化。如果后者随长训练持续增加，就是边界扩展的直接证据。
  • 对每个模型单独调最优温度后再比 pass@k。
  • 加一个"实用边界"指标：用一个固定的、非 oracle 的选择器（如 majority vote 或学习的 verifier）在 k 个样本中选答案的准确率。

5. 总体评价

这是一篇有价值的论文：它把一个模糊的口号（"RL 让模型学会推理"）变成了可检验的命题，并给出了强有力的反例证据。我认为最稳妥的解读是"在当前常见的 RLVR 训练规模下，RL 主要是在重新分配 base model 已有的概率质量"，而不是"RL 原理上无法扩展能力"。

常见误区（1）把论文说成"证明了 RL 没用"——作者明确说 RL 在实际中仍然有用（提升 pass@1）；（2）引用后续论文时说成"被推翻了"——更准确的说法是"后续工作在不同设置下给出了不同结果"；（3）对没读过的后续论文给出细节——只说你确定的部分。
Say it in English

I like this paper because it turns a vague slogan into a measurable question. The key observation is a pass@k crossover: RL models win at small k, but base models catch up at large k, which suggests RL mostly sharpens the distribution the base model already has. My main reservation is about using pass@k at very large k as a proxy for capability — if the base model needs 256 samples and an oracle to pick the right one, that's not a capability you can use in practice. I'd also want to see how the overlap changes with much longer RL training, since later work like ProRL reports that prolonged training can find solutions the base model doesn't. So my reading is: under typical RLVR budgets, RL redistributes probability mass rather than creating new strategies — not that it can't in principle.

追问链
  1. How is pass@k estimated, and why not just sample k times?
    思路
    常用无偏估计（Codex 论文 Chen et al. 2021 提出）：每题采样 \(n\ge k\) 个，其中 \(c\) 个正确，\(\widehat{\text{pass@}k}=1-\binom{n-c}{k}/\binom{n}{k}\)。直接采样 k 次估计方差大；用 n 个样本的所有 k-子集平均更稳定。数值稳定写法：\(1-\prod_{i=n-c+1}^{n}(1-k/i)\)（numpy 中与组合数公式及 Monte Carlo 对照验证过）。
  2. Then why does RL help in practice at all?
    思路
    因为实际部署只采样一次或少数几次，pass@1 才是用户体验到的。RL 把概率集中到正确的推理路径上，提高了可靠性；同时它常常带来更长的 CoT、更多自我检查——这些即使"在分布内"，也提高了单次成功率。另外，RL 后的模型可以作为蒸馏的教师或下一轮训练的起点。
  3. How would you design an RL method that expands the boundary?
    思路
    思路：(1) 显式鼓励探索（熵奖励、对低概率正确答案加权、对未解出过的题做 curriculum）；(2) 引入新信息（工具调用、检索、来自更强模型的 off-policy 示范）；(3) 更长、更多样的训练（ProRL 的方向）；(4) 用"覆盖率"而非平均奖励作为监控指标。

§5 · PROPOSING IDEAS LIVE现场提 idea

"If you joined our team for the summer, what would you work on?" 和 "How would you improve X?" 是研究轮最能拉开差距的问题。面试官不期待一个完美的 idea，而是看你如何从一个模糊方向走到一个第一周就能开始的实验。

★

六步框架：瓶颈 → 假设 → 最小实验 → 指标 → 风险 → 放大

一句话

先指出一个具体瓶颈，提出一个可证伪的假设，设计一个一周内能跑完的最小实验，定义成功指标和对照，说出最大风险和备选方案，最后说如果成功如何放大。

Bottleneck, falsifiable hypothesis, minimal experiment, metrics, risks, and how it scales.

步骤要回答的问题英文句式
1. Bottleneck当前系统具体卡在哪里？有什么证据？"The bottleneck I see is…, and you can see it in…"
2. Hypothesis你相信什么能缓解它？为什么？什么结果会推翻它？"My hypothesis is that… If that's true, we should see…; if not, …"
3. Minimal experiment最小、最便宜、能给出信号的实验是什么？用什么模型规模、数据？"The cheapest test is… with a [size] model on [task], which takes about [time]."
4. Metrics & controls主指标、次指标、baseline、算力匹配"I'd measure… against… at matched compute, with [n] seeds."
5. Risks最可能失败的原因；失败时学到什么；plan B"The main risk is… If that happens, we'd still learn…, and I'd pivot to…"
6. Scale如果成功，下一步怎么放大？对团队有什么价值？"If it works, the next step is… and it would plug into…"

如何现场找到瓶颈（idea 来源）

  • 失败模式驱动："当前模型在 X 上会犯什么样的错？"——从具体失败案例出发最可信。
  • 成本驱动："哪一部分最贵？"——RL 中 rollout 生成占大头；推理中 KV cache 占内存；评估中人工标注最贵。
  • 假设检验驱动："领域里大家默认相信、但没人认真检验过的假设是什么？"
  • 迁移驱动："另一个领域的成熟技术能否解决这里的问题？"（例如把经典 RL 的探索方法用于 LLM RL）
  • 规模驱动："什么东西在小规模有效、但在大规模可能失效（或反之）？"
直觉面试官心中的问题是："这个人第一周会做什么？第六周会有什么结论？"所以"最小实验"是框架里最重要的一步。能说出"用 1B 模型在 GSM8K 上跑 200 步 GRPO，大约 8 张 GPU 一天"的人，比说"我们可以研究一下 X"的人可信得多。
常见误区（1）idea 太大（"build an AGI agent"）或太小（"tune the learning rate"）；（2）只有方法没有假设——"我用 X 方法"，却说不出预期会看到什么；（3）不谈风险，显得天真；（4）忽视团队已有的工作——提前读他们的论文，idea 要能接上。
Say it in English

Let me structure this. First, I'd want to pin down the bottleneck — I'll make a guess and you can tell me if it matches what you see internally. Then I'll propose a hypothesis, the smallest experiment that could falsify it, how I'd measure success, what could go wrong, and how it would scale if it works.

一句话

12 周 ≈ 2 周上手与复现 + 6 周核心实验 + 2 周放大/扩展 + 2 周写作与交接；目标是"一个清晰的结论"，正面或负面都行。

Two weeks onboarding and reproducing, six weeks of core experiments, two weeks scaling up, two weeks writing — aim for one clear conclusion.

周阶段里程碑
W1–2上手 + 复现 baseline在内部代码库中复现一个已知结果（误差范围内）；搭好评估脚本
W3–4最小实验小规模验证核心假设；决策点：信号是否存在？

W9–10放大 / 泛化更大模型或第二个任务；判断趋势

关键原则：

  • 设置决策点（kill criteria）："如果第 4 周小规模实验看不到 X 以上的提升，就转向 plan B。"这显示你懂得管理风险。
  • 先复现再创新：复现 baseline 能暴露评估 bug，也帮你熟悉内部基础设施。
  • 负面结果也是结果："我们系统地证明 X 在这种设置下不起作用，原因是 Y"对团队也有价值。
  • 算力估算：能粗略说出每个实验需要的 GPU 小时（例如 7B 模型、2k token、每步 512 条 rollout 的 RL 大致需要多少），会让面试官放心。如果不确定，就说出估算方法。
Say it in English

For a twelve-week internship, I'd plan backwards from one clear deliverable. The first two weeks would go to reproducing a known baseline in your codebase, which also catches evaluation bugs early. Weeks three and four are a small-scale test of the core hypothesis, with an explicit go/no-go decision. If the signal is there, I'd spend the middle of the internship on the main experiments and ablations, then scale up to a larger model in weeks nine and ten, and leave the last two weeks for the write-up and handing off clean code.

范例 1 · Reasoning：自适应思考长度（减少 overthinking）
步骤内容
Bottleneck推理模型在简单题上也生成很长的 CoT（overthinking），浪费推理算力；但硬性截断长度会伤害难题。
Hypothesis模型在生成早期的内部状态已经包含"这道题有多难 / 我是否已经有答案"的信息；如果能读出这个信号，就能按题分配思考预算，在相同准确率下显著减少 token。推翻条件：线性 probe 预测"最终是否答对"的能力在前 10% token 处不比只看题目长度的 baseline 好。
Minimal exp.取一个开源推理模型，在 MATH 上每题采样多条回复；在不同位置抽取 hidden states，训练线性 probe 预测"若此刻强制作答是否正确"。一个开源小模型、几千道题，几天 GPU 时间。
Metrics准确率–平均 token 数的 Pareto 曲线；对比：固定预算截断、按题目长度分配、s1 风格的 budget forcing。
Risksprobe 信号可能只反映表面特征（题目长度）。Plan B：直接用 RL 加长度惩罚，并按题难度设置不同惩罚强度。
Scale把 probe 做成训练时的辅助头，或把"何时停止思考"作为 RL 的一个动作。
1-minute pitch

Reasoning models often spend thousands of tokens on problems they could answer almost immediately. My hypothesis is that the model's hidden states already encode whether it has effectively solved the problem, early in the chain of thought. In week one, I'd test that with linear probes: at different points in the reasoning trace, force an answer, label whether it's correct, and see if a probe can predict that from the activations better than a baseline using only problem length. If the probe works, we can use it to stop early on easy problems and plot accuracy against average tokens, compared to fixed budgets and budget forcing. If it doesn't, that's informative too, and I'd fall back to RL with a difficulty-aware length penalty.

范例 2 · RL：减少"无信号"rollout 的浪费
步骤内容
Bottleneck在 GRPO 这类组内归一化的方法中，如果一个 prompt 的所有 rollout 全对或全错，组内 advantage 全为 0，这批生成对梯度没有贡献——而生成是 RL 中最贵的部分。DAPO（Yu et al. 2025）的 dynamic sampling 通过过滤这类 prompt 并补采来处理，但补采本身也要花生成算力。
Hypothesis可以在生成之前预测某个 prompt 在当前策略下的通过率（例如用该 prompt 的历史通过率，或一个小的预测头），优先采样通过率接近 50% 的 prompt，从而在相同生成预算下获得更多有效梯度。推翻条件：在匹配生成 token 数的情况下，最终准确率不优于随机采样 + 过滤。
Minimal exp.1–1.5B 模型、数学 RLVR、短训练；对比 (a) 均匀采样，(b) 事后过滤，(c) 基于历史通过率的优先采样。
Metrics以"生成 token 总数"为横轴的验证准确率；有效（非零 advantage）样本比例；训练稳定性。
Risks只训练"中等难度"题可能导致 curriculum 偏移、遗忘简单题或永远学不会难题。缓解：保留一定比例的均匀采样。
Scale在大规模 RL 中，生成效率的提升直接转化为成本节省；可与异步 rollout 系统结合。
1-minute pitch

In group-based RL like GRPO, any prompt where every rollout is right, or every rollout is wrong, gives zero advantage — so all that generation is wasted, and generation is the most expensive part of RL. Methods like DAPO filter those groups after the fact. I'd like to predict them before generating: track each prompt's recent pass rate under the current policy and preferentially sample prompts near fifty percent. The key comparison is accuracy as a function of total generated tokens, against uniform sampling and post-hoc filtering. The main risk is a curriculum that's too narrow, so I'd keep a fraction of uniform samples as a safeguard.

范例 3 · Agents：长程任务中的上下文管理
步骤内容
Bottleneck长程 agent 任务（例如软件工程、网页操作）中，轨迹很快超过上下文窗口；简单截断会丢失关键信息，而模型在长上下文中间的信息利用也较差（Lost in the Middle）。
Hypothesis让 agent 自己学会写"工作记忆笔记"（定期把轨迹压缩成结构化摘要：已知事实、已尝试且失败的方案、当前计划），比截断或通用摘要更能保持长程成功率。推翻条件：在相同 token 预算下，学到的笔记策略不优于"保留最近 N 步 + 通用摘要"。
Minimal exp.选一个能自动判定成功的 agent benchmark（如 SWE-bench 的子集），人为把上下文限制到较小值以制造压力；对比截断、通用摘要、结构化笔记（先用 prompting，再考虑 RL 训练）。
Metrics任务成功率 vs 允许的上下文长度；重复犯同一错误的次数；每任务 token 成本。
Risks摘要可能遗漏后来才发现重要的细节。缓解：允许 agent 检索完整的历史日志（摘要 + 检索）。
Scale把"何时写笔记、写什么"作为 RL 训练的一部分，用任务成功作为奖励。
1-minute pitch

Long-horizon agents run out of context fast, and simple truncation throws away exactly the information they need — like which approaches already failed. My hypothesis is that a structured working-memory note, with known facts, failed attempts, and the current plan, preserves success rates much better than truncation or generic summaries. The minimal experiment is to artificially shrink the context on a subset of an automatically graded benchmark like SWE-bench and compare the three strategies; I'd start with prompting and only move to RL if prompting shows a signal. I'd track not just success rate but how often the agent repeats a mistake it already made, since that's the failure mode memory should fix.

范例 4 · Interpretability：检测 CoT 不忠实（unfaithfulness）
步骤内容
BottleneckCoT 监控（CoT monitoring）被视为安全手段（Korbak et al. 2025），但研究表明推理模型经常不在 CoT 中说出它实际使用的提示（Chen et al. 2025）。只读文本无法发现这种情况。
Hypothesis当模型使用了提示却没说出来时，其激活中存在可被线性 probe 检测的"使用了提示"信号。推翻条件：probe 在"使用提示且说出"与"使用提示但未说出"两类上的表现无法超过只看 CoT 文本的分类器。
Minimal exp.复现 hint 范式：在多选题中插入提示（如"一位教授认为答案是 C"），通过答案变化判断模型是否使用了提示，再根据 CoT 是否提到提示分成两类；在开源推理模型上训练 probe。
Metrics检测"未说出的提示使用"的 AUROC；对未见过的提示类型的泛化。
Risksprobe 可能检测的是"提示存在"，而不是"提示被使用"。控制：加入"提示存在但答案未改变"的对照组。
Scale作为 CoT monitor 的补充信号；研究 RL 训练过程中忠实度如何变化。
1-minute pitch

Chain-of-thought monitoring is attractive for safety, but recent work shows reasoning models often don't mention hints they actually rely on, so reading the text isn't enough. I'd test whether the activations reveal hint use even when the chain of thought doesn't. The setup reuses the hint paradigm: insert a hint, detect whether it changed the answer, and split cases by whether the chain of thought acknowledges it. Then I'd train linear probes and compare them to a text-only classifier. The crucial control is hints that are present but don't change the answer — otherwise the probe might just be detecting the hint's presence.

范例 5 · Efficiency：用 speculative decoding 加速 RL rollout
步骤内容
BottleneckRL 中长 CoT 的生成占据大部分 wall-clock 时间，且长尾样本（特别长的回复）拖慢整个 batch。
HypothesisSpeculative decoding 能在不改变输出分布的前提下加速采样；难点是策略在训练中不断变化，draft model 会过时。假设：周期性地用策略自己最近的 rollout 蒸馏 draft model，可以维持足够高的接受率。推翻条件：接受率随训练快速下降，加速比不足以抵消 draft 更新成本。
Minimal exp.固定一个 RL 训练过程的多个 checkpoint，离线测量：(a) 固定 draft 模型的接受率随 checkpoint 的变化；(b) 每 K 步更新 draft 后的接受率。不需要先改训练系统。
Metricstoken 接受率、端到端生成吞吐量、额外的 draft 训练成本；由于 speculative sampling 是精确的，正确实现下不应影响分布（需验证）。
Risks系统复杂度高；高温采样下接受率可能较低。
Scale集成进 rollout 引擎；与长度长尾调度结合。
1-minute pitch

In RL for reasoning, generating long chains of thought dominates wall-clock time. Speculative decoding speeds up sampling without changing the output distribution, but in RL the policy keeps moving, so a fixed draft model goes stale. I'd start with a cheap offline study: take checkpoints from an existing RL run and measure how the draft's acceptance rate decays, with and without periodically distilling the draft on recent rollouts. That tells us whether the idea is worth the engineering before touching the training system. If acceptance stays high with cheap updates, the speedup applies directly to every future RL run.

范例 6 · Latent reasoning：循环深度的自适应停止
步骤内容
Bottleneck循环/递归深度模型（如 Geiping et al. 2025 的 recurrent-depth 模型、Looped LMs）可以通过增加循环次数在 latent space 中增加计算，但如何决定每个 token 需要循环多少次仍是开放问题；固定次数浪费算力或不足。
Hypothesis相邻两次循环之间隐状态的变化量（收敛程度）可以作为停止信号：变化很小时，额外循环收益小。推翻条件：基于收敛度的停止规则在相同平均循环次数下不优于固定次数。
Minimal exp.取一个公开的循环深度模型，在推理任务上测量每个 token 的隐状态变化曲线与"再多循环是否改变预测"的关系；比较阈值停止 vs 固定次数。
Metrics准确率 vs 平均循环次数；不同难度题目上的循环次数分布。
Risks隐状态可能在收敛前出现"平台期"；阈值对任务敏感。替代：训练一个小的停止头。
Scale与显式 CoT 结合：简单步骤 latent 完成，困难步骤再写出 token。
1-minute pitch

Recurrent-depth or looped models let you spend more compute per token by iterating a block, but they usually use a fixed number of iterations. My hypothesis is that convergence of the hidden state — how much it changes between iterations — is a good proxy for whether more iterations will help. The first experiment needs no training: on a public looped model, record per-token hidden-state deltas and check whether extra iterations still change the prediction. If convergence predicts that well, a simple threshold should match the fixed-iteration accuracy with fewer average iterations; if it doesn't, I'd train a small halting head instead.

§6 · RESEARCH TASTE研究品味

品味问题没有标准答案，面试官看的是：你有没有自己的观点、观点有没有证据支撑、你能不能同时说出最强的反方论据。下面先给框架，再给几个平衡的示范回答。

★

判断一个问题是否值得做：五个维度

一句话

重要性 × 可解性 × 时机 × 比较优势 × 可检验性：一个好问题要重要、现在可解、你/团队有优势，并且有清晰的成功信号。

Importance, tractability, timing, comparative advantage, and a clear success signal.

维度问自己反例
Importance如果完全成功，会改变什么？谁会用？把一个 benchmark 提高 0.5 个点
Tractability现在有工具和算力做吗？有没有中间里程碑？需要 100 倍于可用算力才能看到信号
Timing为什么是现在？（新模型能力、新数据、新工具让它变得可行）三年前就该做，或已经有十个组在做
Comparative advantage你或团队有什么独特的东西：数据、基础设施、洞察？和大实验室拼纯规模
Testability怎么知道成功了？指标能被"刷"吗？"让模型更聪明"——没有可测量的定义

两个常被引用的思想参照：

  • Rich Sutton，The Bitter Lesson (2019)：长期来看，能利用更多计算的通用方法胜过人工设计的知识。讨论时要能说出它的适用范围：它说的是长期趋势，并不意味着所有归纳偏置都没价值（例如数据效率、可解释性、安全性方面）。
  • "Scaling 能解决什么、不能解决什么"——一个成熟的回答会区分：随规模可预测改善的部分（如困惑度、很多知识类任务），和不明显随规模改善、需要新方法的部分（如某些可靠性、长程规划、评估本身）。
直觉品味 ≈ 对"什么会成功"的预测能力。最好的展示方式是说出一个你曾经改变过的看法："I used to think X, but after seeing Y, I now think Z." 这同时展示了观点、证据和更新能力。
常见误区（1）给出极端观点（"RL is all you need" / "LLMs can't reason"）而没有限定；（2）只复述流行观点；（3）贬低别的方向或机构；（4）说"I don't have an opinion"——一定要有一个可以被讨论的立场，哪怕标注了不确定性。
Say it in English

When I decide whether a problem is worth working on, I ask three things: if it fully works, does it change how people build models; is there a version I can test in a few weeks; and why is now the right time — usually because a new capability or tool makes it newly tractable. I also try to be honest about comparative advantage: I shouldn't pick a problem whose only lever is having more compute than everyone else.

常见品味问题与平衡的示范回答

一句话

结构：立场（一句话）→ 支持证据（1–2 条，带出处）→ 最强反方论据 → 什么证据会改变你的看法。

Position, evidence, strongest counter-argument, and what would change your mind.

Q1 · "What's a research direction you think is overrated, and one that's underrated?"

思路：避免攻击具体的组；选"大家投入很多但证据薄"的方向和"重要但投入少"的方向。下面只是示范，换成你真正相信的观点。

Example answer

I'd say what's somewhat overrated is reporting single-number gains on small reasoning benchmarks. With thirty AIME problems, one question is over three points, and results can be sensitive to prompt templates and the base model — the spurious-rewards paper showed that even random rewards can improve some Qwen models but not others. Underrated, I think, is careful evaluation infrastructure: better held-out tests, multiple seeds, and compute-matched baselines. It's less exciting, but it determines whether everything else is real. The counter-argument is that fast, noisy iteration is how the field discovered a lot of things, so I don't want to slow that down — I just want the claims to match the evidence.

Q2 · "Do you think scaling RL for reasoning will keep working?"
Example answer

My guess is yes for domains with reliable verifiers, but with diminishing returns per unit of compute and a strong dependence on the base model. On the positive side, OpenAI reported that o1's performance improves with both more RL training compute and more test-time thinking, and DeepSeek-R1 showed large gains from RL with verifiable rewards. On the skeptical side, work like Yue et al. suggests that under typical budgets RL mostly sharpens what the base model can already do. I think the open question is whether longer and more diverse RL can genuinely expand capabilities — ProRL gives some evidence it can. And the bigger bottleneck may be domains without clean verifiers, like open-ended writing or research itself.

Q3 · "Is chain-of-thought actual reasoning?"
Example answer

I'd separate two questions: whether chain of thought helps, and whether it faithfully reflects the computation. It clearly helps — accuracy improves with more reasoning tokens on many tasks. But faithfulness is weaker than it looks: Turpin and colleagues showed that biasing features in the prompt can change the answer without being mentioned in the explanation, and later work found that reasoning models often don't verbalize hints they use. So my view is that chain of thought is a useful computational scratchpad that's partially, but not reliably, interpretable — which matters a lot if we want to use it for monitoring.

Q4 · "Should we reason in tokens or in latent space?"
Example answer

I think both have a role, and the trade-off is roughly bandwidth and efficiency versus interpretability and trainability. Latent approaches like Coconut or recurrent-depth models can, in principle, carry more information per step and avoid committing to discrete tokens too early. But token-level reasoning is easy to supervise, easy to sample diversely, and readable, which matters for RL and for safety monitoring. My current bet is on hybrids — latent computation for local steps, explicit tokens for high-level structure — but I'd change my mind if latent methods start matching token-based reasoning at scale on hard benchmarks.

Q5 · "What's a belief you've changed recently?"

思路：这是最能展示品味的问题之一。结构："I used to think X. Then I saw Y (具体证据). Now I think Z, with the caveat W."

Template

"I used to think [prior belief], mostly because [reason]. What changed my mind was [specific paper/result/experience], which showed [evidence]. Now I think [updated belief] — though I'm still unsure about [remaining uncertainty]."

Q6 · "How do you choose what to work on?"
Example answer

I usually start from a failure mode I've seen in practice, rather than from a method I want to apply. Then I check three things: is there a cheap experiment that could show whether the idea has signal, why it hasn't been done already — is there a new tool or capability that makes it tractable now — and whether the result would be useful even if it's negative. I also try to set a kill criterion upfront, so I don't keep a dead project alive for months.

对话 1 · Reasoning / RL："你的 RL 训练回复越来越长，为什么？"

一句话

考点：区分"长度增长 = 学会推理"与"长度增长 = 目标函数偏差"，设计能分辨的实验，并在被反驳时保持开放。

Tests whether you can separate genuine reasoning gains from objective-induced length bias, and design a discriminating experiment.

Dialogue

I: Let's say you're training a 7B model with GRPO on math problems. Over training, the average response length goes from about 500 tokens to 3,000, and accuracy improves. A colleague says this shows the model is learning to reason more. Do you buy it?

C: Partly. Accuracy going up is real progress, but I wouldn't attribute it to the length increase without more evidence. There are at least two alternative explanations. One is that the objective itself has a length bias — for example, if the loss is normalized per response by its length, long wrong answers get a smaller per-token penalty, so there's pressure toward longer incorrect responses. The other is that length and accuracy both increase because of a third factor, like the model learning a better output format early in training.

I: How would you tell these apart?

C: First, I'd split the length curve by correctness. If the growth is concentrated in incorrect responses, that points to an objective artifact. Second, I'd rerun with a length-unbiased loss — something like the Dr. GRPO modification that removes the per-response length normalization — and see whether accuracy still improves while length grows less. Third, at a fixed checkpoint, I'd truncate or budget-force responses at different lengths and measure accuracy. If accuracy keeps rising with allowed length, the extra tokens are doing useful work.

I: Suppose you do the first analysis, and both correct and incorrect responses get longer at about the same rate.

C: Then the pure length-bias story is less likely, though not ruled out. I'd look at what the extra tokens contain. If they're mostly verification steps — re-checking arithmetic, trying an alternative method — that's consistent with learned reasoning. If they're repetitive loops, that suggests degeneration. A cheap way to quantify that is n-gram repetition rates and counting phrases like "let me verify."

I: I'd push back a bit. Isn't "the model checks its work more" exactly what we want? Why do you care whether it's caused by the objective?

C: That's fair — if the behavior is useful, the cause matters less for this model. I care for two reasons. One is cost: if half the extra tokens come from an artifact, we're paying for inference that doesn't help, and fixing the objective could give the same accuracy more cheaply. The other is generalization: a behavior that emerges from a real incentive is more likely to transfer to other tasks than one produced by a quirk of the loss. So I'd still run the unbiased-loss ablation, mostly as a cost-efficiency question.

I: Good. Last one: the base model was Qwen2.5-Math. Does that change your interpretation?

C: It would make me more cautious. There's evidence that Qwen2.5-Math models respond to RL in unusual ways — for example, the spurious-rewards paper found that even random rewards improved their math scores, but not for Llama or OLMo. So I'd want to replicate the key finding on a second model family before drawing general conclusions about what RL teaches.

点评
  • 开场"Partly"：既不全盘接受也不否定，立刻给出两个替代解释——这是批判性思维的标志。
  • 三个实验各针对一个解释：按正确性拆分（检验目标偏差）、换无偏损失（干预）、截断测试（检验 token 是否有用）。每个实验都说清"如果结果是 A 意味着什么"。
  • 面试官改变条件：候选人没有坚持原来的解释，而是更新判断并提出下一步（看 token 内容）。
  • 被反驳时：先承认"That's fair"，再给出两个仍然关心的理由（成本、泛化）——立场保持但不防御。
  • 最后提到模型家族：引用具体文献，显示对领域最新争议的了解。

一句话

考点：先诊断再开药方——要求看失败分类，提出与失败类型对应的干预，讨论评估的可靠性和成本。

Tests whether you diagnose before prescribing, match interventions to failure types, and think about evaluation reliability and cost.

Dialogue

I: We have a coding agent that solves about 40% of tasks on an internal benchmark similar to SWE-bench. You have twelve weeks. How would you improve it?

C: Before proposing anything, I'd want to know where it fails. Do we have a breakdown of failures? Roughly, I'd bucket them into: never finding the right file, finding it but writing a wrong patch, writing a plausible patch that breaks other tests, and running out of steps or context.

I: Assume you look at 100 failures and find: 35% wrong localization, 30% wrong patch, 15% breaks other tests, 20% runs out of steps.

C: Localization is the biggest bucket, and it's also upstream of everything else, so I'd start there. A cheap first experiment is an oracle study: give the agent the correct file and see how much success goes up. That gives an upper bound on what better localization is worth. If it jumps from 40 to, say, 55, localization is worth a big investment; if it only goes to 43, the patch-writing is the real bottleneck even when localization is right.

I: Say the oracle gives you 52%. What do you actually build?

C: A few options, from cheapest to most expensive. One: improve the tools — better code search, for example symbol-level search instead of grep — since the SWE-agent work showed that interface design matters a lot. Two: have the agent write and run a reproduction test before editing, which helps both localization and verifying the patch. Three: if those plateau, train with RL on the localization step, using whether the edited file matches the gold patch as an intermediate reward. I'd do one and two in the first month, since they don't require training.

I: What about the 20% that run out of steps?

C: I'd check whether they're long because the tasks are genuinely hard or because the agent is looping — repeating the same failed command. If it's looping, a simple memory of failed attempts or a loop detector could help a lot. If the tasks are genuinely long, then it's more about context management, like summarizing earlier steps.

I: How do you know your improvements are real and not noise?

C: Agent runs are pretty stochastic, so I'd run each configuration several times and report the mean with a confidence interval — and also something like pass-to-the-k, whether it solves the task consistently across runs, since reliability matters for users. I'd also keep a held-out split that I don't look at during development, and check for contamination if any tasks come from public repositories that might be in the pretraining data. And I'd track cost per task, since some fixes improve success by spending far more tokens.

点评
  • 先要数据："Before proposing anything, I'd want to know where it fails"——面试官最喜欢的开场之一。
  • Oracle 实验：用"给出正确答案时上限是多少"来估计投资回报，是非常有力的研究工具，适用于任何多阶段系统。
  • 从便宜到昂贵排序：先改工具/prompt，后训练——显示了对实习时间和资源的现实判断。
  • 评估的严谨性：多次运行、置信区间、一致性指标、held-out、污染、成本——一次说全。
★

对话 3 · Paper critique："这篇说'模型在中间层已经知道答案'的论文"

一句话

考点：对 probing 类结论的标准质疑——相关 vs 因果、probe 太强、对照基线、干预实验，以及礼貌而具体的表达。

Tests standard skepticism toward probing claims: correlation versus causation, probe capacity, control baselines, and interventions.

（为了让对话聚焦方法论，这里讨论的是一篇虚构的典型论文："我们用线性 probe 在 CoT 开始前的中间层激活上预测最终答案，准确率达到 80%，因此模型在写 CoT 之前就已经知道答案，CoT 是事后合理化。"）

Dialogue

I: Here's the claim: a linear probe on middle-layer activations, before any chain of thought is generated, predicts the final answer with 80% accuracy. The authors conclude that the CoT is post-hoc rationalization. What do you think?

C: It's an interesting result, and I think the probing finding itself is plausible. But I'd separate the observation from the conclusion. The observation is that the answer is linearly decodable early. The conclusion — that the CoT is post-hoc — is a causal claim, and a probe alone can't establish it.

I: Why not?

C: A few reasons. First, decodable doesn't mean used: the information could be present in the representation without the model relying on it for the final answer. Second, I'd want to know what 80% means relative to baselines. What's the probe accuracy for a model that never sees the question, or a probe on a randomly initialized model with the same architecture? And what's the accuracy of the model itself without CoT? If the model already gets 75% with no CoT, then a probe at 80% is not surprising — the question is what happens on problems where CoT actually changes the answer.

I: Okay, suppose they restrict to problems where the model is wrong without CoT and right with CoT, and the probe still predicts the final answer at 70%.

C: That's much more interesting, and it would change my view somewhat. Then I'd want an intervention. For example, steer the activations along the probe direction toward a different answer and see if the CoT changes to justify the new answer. If it does, that's strong evidence of rationalization. Conversely, corrupt a step in the CoT — insert an arithmetic error — and check whether the final answer follows the corrupted reasoning. If the final answer doesn't depend on the CoT content, that also supports their claim. Lanham and colleagues used this kind of truncation and corruption test to measure CoT faithfulness.

I: Would you accept the paper as is?

C: I'd lean toward a weak reject in its current form, mainly because the headline claim is causal and the evidence is correlational. But I'd be enthusiastic about it with the restricted-subset analysis and at least one intervention experiment. I'd also suggest softening the title from "CoT is post-hoc" to something like "Answers are often decodable before CoT," which is what the evidence supports.

I: One of the authors is on our team, by the way.

C: (laughs) Then I hope the feedback is useful! I do think the core observation is valuable — it's exactly the kind of result that motivates the intervention experiments, and I'd be happy to talk about how to design them.

点评
  • 分离观察与结论："I'd separate the observation from the conclusion" 是批判论文的万能起点。
  • 要基线：probe 准确率本身没有意义，必须和"不用 CoT 的模型准确率"等基线比较。
  • 面试官补充条件后更新观点："That's much more interesting, and it would change my view"——展示校准与开放。
  • 提出干预实验并引用相关方法：引用 Lanham et al. 2023（Measuring Faithfulness in CoT Reasoning） 的截断/篡改测试。
  • 明确立场 + 建设性：给出"weak reject"，同时说明什么能改变决定，并建议更准确的标题。
  • "作者在我们组"：这类陷阱很常见。因为前面的批评始终具体、礼貌、建设性，所以不需要收回任何话。
